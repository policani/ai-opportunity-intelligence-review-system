# Build Buy Hire Wait Routing

Use these route definitions:

- **Prototype:** The value hypothesis is plausible, the workflow is describable, and a small proof can test the claim cheaply.
- **Build:** The workflow is differentiated, strategically important, cost drivers are understood, and available tools do not solve the important part.
- **Buy:** The workflow is common, vendor solutions exist, and differentiation comes from adoption, controls, and process fit rather than custom build.
- **Automate with existing tools:** Current tools can solve enough of the problem with configuration, scripts, workflow packaging, or rules-based automation.
- **Hire or upskill:** The gap is human capability, ownership, evaluation design, workflow engineering, change leadership, or support capacity.
- **Wait:** Market, model, vendor, data, cost, or control maturity is too unstable for committed investment.
- **Process-first:** The workflow is too unclear, inconsistent, or broken for AI to help safely.
- **Decompose further:** The idea bundles multiple workflows or lacks enough clarity to route.
- **Stop:** The problem, value, owner, cost case, or evidence is too weak to justify more attention.

Always include route rationale, the next smallest useful action, and the reason
the recommended route is cheaper or safer than the nearest alternative.
